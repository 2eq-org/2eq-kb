# Central Claim

## TL;DR
- This repository centers on the **Singular Motivational Subversion Hypothesis (SMSH)**.
- The claim here is the **minimal** form: existence and functional effect.
- Claims about ease, frequency, or safe scalability of “removal” are **explicitly bracketed**.
- The most important companion page is **Predictions & Falsifiers**.

---

## What this page is
A precise statement of the hypothesis, its boundaries, and what it implies at a high level.

## What this page is not
- Not a claim of scientific consensus  
- Not an argument that anyone should “accept” the hypothesis  
- Not a self-help framework or therapeutic protocol  
- Not a claim that dramatic psychological shifts are common, safe, or reproducible  

---

## The Singular Motivational Subversion Hypothesis (SMSH)

### Minimal statement
In the average human, there exists a **persistent, self-stabilizing internal dynamic** that systematically distorts motivation, valuation, attention, and moral cognition.

This dynamic is hypothesized to be:
- **functionally singular** (unitary at the level of effect, despite diverse surface expressions),
- **energetically defended** (resistant to disruption or conscious inspection),
- and **equilibrium-forming** (tending to reassert itself after perturbation).

### What SMSH is trying to explain
SMSH is proposed to explain a cluster of otherwise difficult-to-unify phenomena, including:
- persistent self-contradiction without confusion,
- relapse after insight or improvement,
- chronic dissatisfaction that survives external wins,
- and cross-cultural redundancy in coping/defense patterns.

---

## Scope boundaries (what is *not* claimed here)

This repository does **not** assume:
- that the hypothesized dynamic is easy to remove,
- that it can be removed safely,
- that it can be removed reliably or at scale,
- or that reported “shift events” constitute evidence.

The existence claim is treated as logically separable from any removal claim.

---

## Key implications if the hypothesis is even partially correct
If SMSH is substantially correct, it implies that many downstream interventions may be **ceiling-limited** by operator distortion, including:
- mental health progress,
- institutional/governance improvement,
- long-horizon coordination (e.g., climate and global risk),
- and potentially AI alignment (via human motivational misalignment).

These implications remain speculative until the hypothesis is tested and discriminated from alternatives.
