# 2EQ Knowledge Base

## What this is
This site is a structured, test-oriented knowledge base for evaluating the **Singular Motivational Subversion Hypothesis (SMSH)** and related claims within the broader 2EQ framework.

The central hypothesis proposes that most humans operate under a persistent, self-stabilizing internal dynamic that systematically distorts motivation, valuation, attention, and moral cognition. This repository exists to make that hypothesis **legible, inspectable, and falsifiable**.

This is **not** a self-help program, not a belief system, and not a claim of established scientific consensus.

---

## What this is not
- Not a therapeutic method or personal development course  
- Not a recruitment or conversion effort  
- Not an assertion that the hypothesis is proven  
- Not a claim that any reported psychological shifts are common or safely reproducible  

---

## How to use this repository
Start with:
- **Definitions** — controlled vocabulary used consistently throughout
- **Central Claim** — the minimal form of the hypothesis and its scope limits
- **Predictions & Falsifiers** — what would weaken or disprove the claim
- **Skeptical Case** — a steelman critique written from a hostile-but-honest perspective
- **Case Reports** — anecdotal reports, clearly labeled as non-evidence

The structure is designed to support **serious evaluation**, not persuasion.

---

## Ethical posture
This project adopts the following principles:

- Investigate, do not believe  
- Test, do not preach  
- Separate hypotheses from anecdotes  
- Avoid moral hierarchy or identity formation  
- Prioritize falsification over elaboration  

The primary ethical risk is **premature certainty**, not skepticism.

---

## Repository structure
This knowledge base contains:

- `/definitions/` — canonical glossary and term usage  
- `/central-claim/` — precise statement of the hypothesis and boundaries  
- `/predictions-falsifiers/` — explicit tests and disconfirming evidence  
- `/common-confusions/` — known failure modes and misinterpretations  
- `/case-reports/` — anecdotal phenomenology (clearly labeled)  
- `/skeptical-case/` — strongest objections and critiques  
- `/docs/` — source documents and archival materials
