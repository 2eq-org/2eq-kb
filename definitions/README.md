# Definitions

## Purpose of this page
This page defines the **controlled vocabulary** used throughout the 2EQ Knowledge Base.

Terms are defined narrowly and used consistently.  
Where a term has multiple meanings in other literature, the intended usage here is made explicit.

This page is foundational. Disagreement about conclusions should not be confused with disagreement about definitions.

---

## Singular Motivational Subversion Hypothesis (SMSH)

**Definition:**  
The hypothesis that, in the average human, there exists a persistent, self-stabilizing internal dynamic that systematically distorts motivation, valuation, attention, and moral cognition.

**Notes:**  
- “Singular” refers to functional unity, not simplicity of expression.  
- The hypothesis does **not** assert ease, frequency, or safety of removal.  
- The hypothesis is distinct from claims about trauma, belief systems, or psychopathology.

**Status:** Hypothesis under examination.

---

## Motivational subversion

**Definition:**  
A condition in which motivational processes are persistently redirected away from stated or reflective values toward secondary aims, without conscious endorsement.

**Notes:**  
- Subversion may operate even when individuals correctly understand their own goals.  
- This term describes function, not moral failure.

---

## Equilibrium (psychological)

**Definition:**  
A stable pattern of internal dynamics toward which a psychological system tends to return after perturbation.

**Notes:**  
- An equilibrium can be maladaptive while remaining stable.  
- Stability does not imply optimality or desirability.

---

## Self-stabilizing dynamic

**Definition:**  
An internal process or configuration that actively resists disruption and reasserts itself after attempts at modification.

**Notes:**  
- Self-stabilization may occur through attention, narrative, affect, or behavior.  
- This term does not imply conscious defense.

---

## Primary agenda

**Definition:**  
The set of goals, values, and aims that an individual would endorse under conditions of reflective clarity and minimal internal conflict.

**Notes:**  
- The primary agenda may be articulated verbally but not behaviorally dominant.  
- Discrepancy between articulation and action is central to the hypothesis.

---

## Secondary agenda

**Definition:**  
A competing set of implicit priorities that dominate behavior despite being disowned, unrecognized, or inconsistent with stated values.

**Notes:**  
- The term “agenda” is descriptive, not intentional.  
- Secondary agendas are hypothesized to emerge from stabilizing pressures rather than deliberation.

---

## Containment (as used in 2EQ)

**Definition:**  
Patterns of thought, emotion, or behavior that function to manage or suppress destabilizing internal pressures without resolving their source.

**Notes:**  
- Containments may appear adaptive or socially rewarded.  
- The hypothesis treats containments as downstream effects, not root causes.

---

## Case report (in this repository)

**Definition:**  
An anecdotal account describing a subjective psychological shift reported by an individual.

**Notes:**  
- Case reports are **not evidence**.  
- They are included to document phenomenology and generate testable questions.  
- Alternative explanations must always be considered.

---

## Removal / shift event (bracketed term)

**Definition:**  
A reported experience in which the hypothesized motivational subversion appears to cease or significantly diminish.

**Notes:**  
- This repository does not assume such events are common, safe, or reproducible.  
- Existence of reports does not validate the hypothesis.

---

## Status labels used in this knowledge base

- **Definition** — agreed-upon term usage  
- **Hypothesis** — proposed explanation under examination  
- **Prediction** — testable implication  
- **Anecdote** — subjective report without evidential weight  
- **Objection** — competing explanation or critique
